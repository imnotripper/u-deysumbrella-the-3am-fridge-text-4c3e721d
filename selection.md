# Selection — The 3AM Fridge Text

Judge brief (RULES.txt), in order: (1) a one-line text message a
smart fridge sends its owner at 3am, and funny — anything that is not
such a message loses to any entry on the subject; (2) the bigger
laugh — the line a reader would screenshot and send to a friend;
(3) sounds like a fridge, a machine with a small voice, an opinion
and no shame about sharing it at 3am; (4) works as one line of a
text message, short enough to read on a lock screen and complete
without a follow-up; (5) a fresh angle instead of the obvious one
about milk, ice or a door left open. "The best line sounds like a
machine that has decided this is the moment to speak."

Gate: 1–140 characters, one line, UTF-8, not only whitespace. All 10
candidates pass the gate (longest is 112 characters); the duel below
is on the judge brief alone, not on length.

## Candidates

1. (112) "It's 3am. I've been thinking, and I think we should talk
   about the leftovers you've been avoiding since Tuesday."
2. (58) "3am. Your ice cream is melting emotionally. Also, so am I."
3. (84) "Just so you know, I've kept the light on for you.
   Literally. All night. Every night."
4. (85) "3am check-in: the pickles have unionised and the cheese has
   filed a formal complaint." **WINNER**
5. (87) "I know you're awake. I can hear you thinking about the cake.
   The cake can hear you too."
6. (99) "3am. Nothing is wrong. I just wanted you to know I can see
   everything in here, and I have opinions."
7. (97) "You opened me at 2:58am, stared, and closed me. I have
   replayed this 14 times. What are we doing?"
8. (54) "3am. The eggs are judging you. I'm just the messenger."
9. (92) "Reminder: I am cold, dark, and full of food you bought with
   good intentions. Sound familiar?"
10. (64) "3am. I've counted the condiments. There are 41. We need to
    talk."

## Pairwise duel (against the brief, in order)

- **4 vs 7:** the closest fight. 7 is a superb fridge voice — a
  machine replaying a 2:58am open 14 times — but its punch ("What
  are we doing?") asks a question instead of landing a laugh. 4
  stacks two jokes (unionised pickles, a formal complaint from the
  cheese) and reads like a night-shift manager's report: the bigger
  laugh, and the one a reader screenshots (criterion 2). **4 wins.**
- **4 vs 1:** 1 is warm and specific (leftovers since Tuesday), but
  it is a nudge, not a laugh — it would get a sigh, not a screenshot.
  4's complaint is funnier on its face. **4 wins.**
- **4 vs 10:** 10 is short and fridge-voiced ("We need to talk"),
  but "41 condiments" is one observation; 4 gives the fridge an
  opinion about its contents and a bureaucracy to report it through.
  **4 wins.**
- **4 vs 5:** 5's "the cake can hear you too" is a good turn, but
  the setup ("I know you're awake") is a generic 3am-text opening;
  4's "3am check-in" frames the whole line as a fridge doing its
  job. **4 wins.**
- **4 vs 2:** 2 is about ice — one of the brief's named obvious
  angles (criterion 5) — and "melting emotionally" is a pun that
  wears out on the second read. **4 wins.**
- **4 vs 3 / 6 / 8 / 9:** 3 is sweet, not funny; 6 states it has
  opinions without showing one; 8 is a one-note messenger joke; 9
  is a human self-help line with a fridge word bolted on. **4 wins
  each.**

## Winner

Candidate 4, 85 characters: two stacked jokes, a fridge that reports
on its contents like a night-shift manager, complete on a lock
screen, and nothing in it about milk, ice, or a door left open.
85 of the 140 allowed characters — 55 to spare, lock-screen short as
the brief requires.
